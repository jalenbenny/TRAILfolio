## Courses Taught

My teaching at Loyola University Chicago includes graduate-level courses in health informatics, data science, and public health:

- **MPBH 412: Data Management and Statistical Computing for Public Health.** Data management, transformation, statistical computing, and reporting using SAS.
- **HIDS 422: Ontologies in Health.** Knowledge representation, clinical terminologies, ontologies, and their applications in health information systems.
- **HIDS 431: Introduction to Natural Language Processing in Health.** Computational methods for processing unstructured clinical text, information extraction, and healthcare applications of NLP.
- **HIDS 399/499: Health Informatics Capstone.** Supervision of applied research projects addressing problems in healthcare through informatics and data science.

In addition to formal coursework, I have delivered invited lectures and seminars at national and international institutions on applications of artificial intelligence in healthcare, particularly clinical decision support and medication management.

## Research Mentoring

I mentor undergraduate and graduate students, doctoral candidates, and postdoctoral researchers with backgrounds in computer science, health informatics, medicine, nursing, public health, and engineering. My mentoring activities are closely integrated with my research program, providing trainees with opportunities to work on interdisciplinary projects involving clinical AI, natural language processing, clinical decision support, critical care informatics, emergency medical services, and health equity.

My approach to mentoring combines close scientific collaboration with increasing research independence. I work with trainees on formulating research questions, developing methodological approaches, evaluating analytical results, and interpreting findings in their clinical and operational contexts. As their projects progress, I encourage them to take greater ownership of the research, including methodological decisions, scientific writing, and dissemination.

An important aspect of my mentoring is exposing trainees to the broader research process beyond model development and data analysis. This includes interdisciplinary collaboration with clinical and research teams, preparation of manuscripts and grant proposals, presentations at scientific meetings, and consideration of how research findings can be translated into clinical practice. I place particular emphasis on methodological rigor, reproducibility, and understanding the practical implications and limitations of analytical approaches.

I also recognize that trainees have different professional goals and levels of experience. Accordingly, I aim to provide mentoring that supports their individual development while preparing them to contribute independently to research, academia, and applied healthcare innovation.

## Research Opportunities

I welcome inquiries from students interested in applying artificial intelligence, data science, statistics, and other quantitative methods to significant problems in healthcare. Research opportunities are aligned with ongoing projects and may involve methodological development, qualitative and translational research, clinical validation, analysis of clinical and healthcare data, or the development and evaluation of AI-based clinical decision support systems.
